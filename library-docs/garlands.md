Rows of swags (lines that hang in curves between hooks) drop into place one row at a time from the top, each row its
own colour, until the layer is dressed; they hold a moment, fade out together and start again. Festive bunting, a
Christmas or fairground look, a theatrical dressing of the façade. Reach for **bars** for straight stripes that move
continuously, **wave** for one line rolling across, **curtain** for a pleated reveal.

## Params
- colours: Each row is flat; `body/accent` alternates two families.
- direction: With `up` the swags arch upward instead of hanging.
- speed: Rows a second. 0.5–1.5 for a calm dressing; a full cycle takes (rows + 2) ÷ speed seconds.
- rows: 3–6 on the whole rig; more rows need a taller, denser layer.
- swags: 2–5; many swags on a narrow layer turn into a zigzag.
- sag: 0.05–0.12.
- width: 0.03–0.06; thinner breaks up on sparse LEDs.

## Pairings
- Blend `over` a dim `colour-wash` bed so the façade is never black between cycles.
- With `sparkle` in `high` above for twinkling bulbs on the swags.
- On `arch-main` and `rose` together for bunting across the front.

## Pitfalls
- Sag deeper than the row spacing (1 ÷ rows) tangles neighbouring rows.
- With many rows a dropping row only appears near its place instead of falling from above.
- At speed 4–5 with few rows the whole layer fades and refills several times a second; watch the flash check.
