What is above it slides in Direction at Speed (layers a second), what leaves one side coming back in at the other.
`round` turns the layer about its centre, `out`/`in` drift it from or to the centre, `along` moves it along every
wire, so a still pattern on the strands creeps up the towers. A warp: it bends the modules above it in its layer up to
a blur or trails, so it goes after the source. Reach for it to set a still source (clouds frozen, a gradient, a
mosaic) in motion, or to add drift to a moving one; **rotate** turns rather than slides, **turbulence** pushes
unevenly, **bars** and **single-strand** draw their own motion.

## Params
- direction: which way it slides: up, down, right, left, back, front, out, in, round, along (each wire from its
  start).
- speed: layers a second, so 1 crosses the whole layer each second; 0.02–0.2 is a drift. Negative reverses.

## Pairings
- After a colour-wash with gradient colours or a smooth bars: a moving gradient across the façade.
- After clouds (speed 0) on `right`: cloud banks blowing past; add **turbulence** for boil.
- With `along` after a still sparkle or garlands: lights creep up every strand.

## Pitfalls
- Wrapping shows a seam where the source's two edges meet, unless the source tiles (bars with whole numbers, plasma).
- Speed above ~1 on a high-contrast source sweeps large areas fast; check the flash check.
- On top of a source that already moves the same way, speeds add; opposite ways they cancel to a standstill.
