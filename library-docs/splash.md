---
looks: Ripples through the wiring wherever the look above suddenly brightens, in its colour.
---
Wherever what is above it suddenly brightens, it kicks water on the rig's neighbour map, and a ripple runs out along
the strands, crosses others and comes back off the ends, in the colour of what set it off. A **sparkle** glint
rings out, a **strobe** hit makes the whole map shiver, a **single-strand** chase pushes a wake ahead of it. Show
puts the ripples over what is above, or alone. Same water as **waves**, set off by the look instead of by drops.

## Params
- speed: LEDs a second along a strand: 10–20 rolls, 40+ races.
- fade: 0.2–0.4 lets ripples run far; 0.7+ keeps them close to what set them off.
- strength: 1 faint rings; 2 clear from across the site; 3 every hit splashes.
- show: `ripples` alone over a sparse source is the cleanest; `both` keeps what is above.

## Pairings
- **strobe** (scatter 1, speed 1) → splash `ripples`: each piece's flash rings out through the wiring in its colour.
- **strobe** on the beat → splash: the map shivers on each hit.
- **sparkle** (how many low, flash short) → splash `ripples`: every glint rings out, faint from afar.
- **single-strand** comets → splash, strength 0.5: a wake ahead and behind.

## Pitfalls
- It reacts only to brightening: a steady or fading source makes no ripples.
- A source that lights most of the layer at once (a full-cover chase, a wash) splashes everywhere and washes to white:
  lower strength or use a sparse source.
- It ends a stage: warps below it don't bend what is above. Seeking or jumping more than a second calms the water.
- On dense pieces (the main arches) ripples meet fast and blur into a grey haze: raise fade or lower strength.
